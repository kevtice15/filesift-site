Worth doing first

Stop the pile from growing

Two macOS settings solve tomorrow's mess before you touch today's:

Give screenshots their own folder

Press ⇧⌘5, click Options, and choose a folder under Save to. New screenshots stop landing on your Desktop and pile up in one place you can triage on your schedule.

Tidy the Desktop view

Right-click your Desktop and choose Use Stacks — everything groups by kind, so the screenshots that are already there at least stop hiding your actual work.

Questions

How do I change where screenshots are saved on a Mac?

Press ⇧⌘5, click Options, and pick a folder under "Save to" — a dedicated Screenshots folder works well. New screenshots land there instead of on your Desktop. This stops the pile from growing; sorting the existing backlog is the other half.

What's the fastest way to sort hundreds of old screenshots?

Triage them visually. In FileSift you point at the folder, set up destinations like Keep, Reference, and Trash, and press one number key per screenshot — each one shows full size, so you can tell the receipts from the memes at a glance. A few hundred screenshots take minutes, and ⌘Z physically undoes any move.

Can I just delete all my screenshots?

Usually not safely — screenshot piles hide receipts, confirmation numbers, and reference images among the disposable ones. That's why a quick visual pass beats a bulk delete: you see each one big and make the keep-or-toss call in under a second.

Do I have to move screenshots into folders, or can I tag them?

Either. A FileSift destination can move the file, apply a macOS Finder tag in place, or both — so you can color-code screenshots where they are instead of relocating them.
